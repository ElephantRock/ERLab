# Rank Stability Under Covariate Perturbation: A Seeded Empirical Study of Ridge and Huber Regression on Airfoil Self-Noise and Concrete Strength

## Abstract

Robust regression methods are routinely ranked by error metrics at a single operating condition, yet deployed tabular models encounter test-time covariate perturbations of unknown magnitude. This paper executes a fully deterministic, seed-frozen benchmark capability (`tabular_robust_regression_v1`) on two tabular regression datasets — airfoil_self_noise and concrete_strength — and asks whether such rankings survive increasing perturbation severity. Two classical linear estimators implemented in pure Python are compared: ridge regression (closed form, lambda = 1.0, intercept unregularized) and Huber regression (iteratively reweighted least squares initialized from the ridge solution, delta = 1.345 times a MAD-based residual scale, exactly 50 iterations), evaluated against a training-mean-predictor reference under seeded Gaussian noise added to standardized test covariates at severities 0.0, 0.25, 0.5, and 0.75. Rankings prove metric- and severity-dependent. On airfoil_self_noise, Huber leads ridge in MAE at severity 0.0 (3.666145 [RESULT-1] versus 3.691782 [RESULT-4]) but ridge leads from severity 0.25 onward (3.741122 [RESULT-10] versus 3.742593 [RESULT-7] at severity 0.25); on concrete_strength the MAE lead changes hands only at severity 0.75 (9.212387 [RESULT-49] versus 9.327266 [RESULT-46]). RMSE and R-squared rankings favor ridge at every severity on both datasets, and both estimators dominate the mean predictor by wide margins (e.g., ridge R-squared 0.35661 [RESULT-23] versus -0.006701 [RESULT-26] on airfoil_self_noise at severity 0.75). A non-monotone MAE response on concrete_strength at severity 0.75 cautions against single-metric model selection under shift. The contribution is deliberately bounded: an auditable empirical note on two datasets and two estimators with per-result provenance, not a new method or a general claim.

## 1. Introduction

Tabular regression models are increasingly deployed in engineering and scientific settings where the statistical properties of inputs at test time differ from those at training time. Conventional benchmark practice scores each candidate model at a single operating condition — an unperturbed test set, or occasionally one fixed corruption level — and hands the winning model to deployment. Yet covariate noise at inference time is rarely under the practitioner's control, and a ranking established at one noise level may not survive transport to another. This paper concerns that transportability question for two classical off-the-shelf linear estimators: ridge regression and Huber M-estimation.

A substantial adjacent literature motivates but does not answer this question. As background, conformal prediction sets for tabular regression under covariate shift provide finite-sample marginal and conditional coverage guarantees, including for gradient-boosted models [SOURCE-1], [SOURCE-3]; parallel work studies regression under randomly censored covariates [SOURCE-7] and covariate-centric model families such as clustered covariate regression [SOURCE-13] and reduced-rank regression [SOURCE-14]. This literature is largely concerned with inference validity and with richer model classes. Robust point estimators such as Huber regression, by contrast, are motivated by loss functions that treat residuals asymmetrically and are typically validated under label contamination at a fixed noise level, not under controlled sweeps of *covariate* perturbation severity with frozen seeds. A ranking between two estimators that flips between two perturbation severities is exactly the kind of instability that single-condition evaluation can conceal, and it is this specific, narrow gap that the present study addresses.

The study executes a controlled, fully deterministic protocol — the `tabular_robust_regression_v1` capability — that sweeps seeded Gaussian covariate perturbation severity across {0.0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75} and evaluates pure-Python implementations of ridge and Huber regression against a training-set mean-predictor reference on two tabular regression datasets: airfoil_self_noise and concrete_strength. Every number is a deterministic function of the data, the seeds, and a frozen method contract; no hyperparameter search is performed and no external machine-learning library is used.

The contributions are bounded to the executed analysis and are stated as such. First, the study documents a frozen, seed-reproducible method contract verbatim, eliminating implementation and tuning variance as confounds; this is an evaluation-protocol contribution, not a new estimator. Second, it provides direct empirical evidence of severity-dependent MAE rank reversals between ridge and Huber regression on both executed datasets, with the reversal threshold differing across datasets (between severities 0.0 and 0.25 on airfoil_self_noise; between 0.5 and 0.75 on concrete_strength). Third, it documents a metric dissociation: RMSE and R-squared rankings remain stable — ridge ahead at every severity on both datasets — while MAE rankings reverse, and a non-monotone MAE response on concrete_strength at severity 0.75 breaks the monotone-degradation intuition. Fourth, every quantitative claim carries a per-result provenance marker, enabling audit. Explicit non-goals: no new method, no new theory, no claim beyond two datasets, two linear estimators, one reference predictor, and one perturbation family; stronger comparators (OLS, Lasso, gradient boosting, Theil–Sen, RANSAC) and hyperparameter ablations are treated as future work, not as demonstrated contributions.

## 2. Related Work

**Covariate shift and corrupted covariates (background).** Work on distribution shift for tabular regression has concentrated largely on inference validity rather than point-prediction ranking. Conformal prediction sets for tabular regression under covariate shift deliver finite-sample marginal and conditional coverage guarantees, including when the underlying regressor is a gradient-boosted model [SOURCE-1], [SOURCE-3]. As additional background, regression under randomly censored covariates [SOURCE-7], clustered covariate regression [SOURCE-13], and reduced-rank regression [SOURCE-14] study covariate-centric model families whose goals (consistent estimation under specific corruption mechanisms, dimension reduction, multitarget structure) are orthogonal to the question asked here. The present study is complementary: it holds two estimators fixed and asks how *evaluation under seeded additive covariate noise* reshapes the ranking between them, which none of the above directly addresses.

**Robust regression and loss–metric alignment (background).** Huber-type M-estimation occupies a classical position in robust statistics: its loss is quadratic for small residuals and linear beyond a threshold delta, trading efficiency under Gaussian errors for insensitivity to outliers (internal reasoning). The delta = 1.345 multiplier corresponds to the conventional Gaussian-efficiency calibration (internal reasoning). This classical literature motivates the pairing studied here, but it does not supply the specific empirical object of interest: a seeded severity sweep over *test-time covariate* perturbations with a frozen contract and per-result provenance. Prior validation of robust estimators has centered on label contamination at fixed noise levels, a different axis entirely (internal reasoning).

**Evaluation and benchmarking practice (motivation).** The study is further motivated by benchmarking practice in applied regression, where engineering datasets such as concrete compressive-strength prediction and aerodynamic self-noise prediction are routinely used to rank methods at a single operating point. Such applications inherit whatever ranking instability exists at deployment-time input noise levels. The finding reported below — that Huber's MAE advantage erodes and reverses at higher perturbation severities on both executed datasets — is a caution for robustness claims transferable across operating conditions. The present results concretize this concern: on both datasets the MAE ranking between ridge and Huber reverses at some severity, while RMSE and R-squared rankings do not. Comparisons against stronger robust estimators (Theil–Sen, RANSAC) or nonlinear regressors (gradient boosting), which appear in the adjacent literature only as the *objects* of coverage analysis [SOURCE-1], [SOURCE-3], were not executed here and remain future work.

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 Problem Definition

Let $(x_i, y_i)_{i=1}^{n}$ denote a tabular regression dataset with covariates $x_i \in \mathbb{R}^{p}$ and label $y_i \in \mathbb{R}$. Rows are partitioned by a deterministic seeded shuffle into training indices $I_{\mathrm{tr}}$ (80%) and test indices $I_{\mathrm{te}}$ (20%), with covariates standardized using training-split statistics. Given a fitted predictor $f$, the study evaluates MAE, RMSE, and $R^2$ on perturbed test covariates

$$\tilde{x}_i^{(s)} = \tilde{x}_i + \varepsilon_i^{(s)}, \qquad \varepsilon_i^{(s)} \sim \mathcal{N}\!\left(0,\; (0.5\,s)^2 I_p\right), \qquad s \in \{0.0,\,0.25,\,0.5,\,0.75\},$$

where $\tilde{x}_i$ is the standardized test covariate vector and $\varepsilon$ is generated deterministically (seed 42 plus the severity index, Box–Muller sampling). Labels are never perturbed. The two fitted models are ridge regression with objective

$$\hat{\beta}_0, \hat{\beta} = \arg\min_{\beta_0, \beta} \; \sum_{i \in I_{\mathrm{tr}}} \left( y_i - \beta_0 - \tilde{x}_i^{\top}\beta \right)^2 + \lambda \|\beta\|_2^2, \qquad \lambda = 1.0,$$

with the intercept $\beta_0$ left unregularized, and Huber M-estimation with loss

$$\rho_{\delta}(r) = \begin{cases} \tfrac{1}{2} r^2, & |r| \le \delta, \\[2pt] \delta\left(|r| - \tfrac{1}{2}\delta\right), & |r| > \delta, \end{cases}$$

fitted by iteratively reweighted least squares (IRLS) with weights $w_i = \min(1, \delta / |r_i|)$, where the residual scale is recomputed each iteration as $s = \max(10^{-8},\, 1.4826 \cdot \operatorname{med}_i |r_i - \operatorname{med}_j r_j|)$ and $\delta = 1.345\, s$ (the conventional Gaussian-efficiency multiplier; internal reasoning). Evaluation metrics are

$$\mathrm{MAE} = \frac{1}{m}\sum_{i \in I_{\mathrm{te}}} \left| f(\tilde{x}_i^{(s)}) - y_i \right|, \qquad \mathrm{RMSE} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i \in I_{\mathrm{te}}} \left( f(\tilde{x}_i^{(s)}) - y_i \right)^2},$$

$$R^2 = 1 - \frac{\sum_{i \in I_{\mathrm{te}}} \left(y_i - f(\tilde{x}_i^{(s)})\right)^2}{\sum_{i \in I_{\mathrm{te}}} \left(y_i - \bar{y}_{\mathrm{te}}\right)^2},$$

with $m = |I_{\mathrm{te}}|$ and $\bar{y}_{\mathrm{te}}$ the test-set mean. Rank stability is assessed by the sign of the Huber-minus-ridge metric gap $\Delta^{(M)}_s = M^{\mathrm{huber}}_s - M^{\mathrm{ridge}}_s$ for each metric $M$ and severity $s$; a rank reversal is a sign change across severity.

### 3.2 Executed Protocol (Frozen Method Contract)

The following contract was executed exactly as written and is reproduced verbatim:

> - Two regression models are fitted on features standardized with training-set statistics. Ridge regression is solved in closed form (lambda = 1.0, with the intercept left unregularized) by pure-Python Gaussian elimination. Huber regression is fitted by iteratively reweighted least squares initialized from the ridge solution, with delta = 1.345 times a MAD-based residual scale (1.4826 x median absolute deviation about the residual median, floored at 1e-8) recomputed each iteration, for exactly 50 iterations. No external machine-learning library is used.
> - The baseline is the training-set mean predictor. Its predictions do not read the covariates, so its MAE, RMSE, and R-squared are reported once per dataset and are identical at every perturbation severity.
> - Covariate perturbation adds deterministic seeded zero-mean Gaussian noise to the standardized test features only, with standard deviation equal to severity x 0.5 at severities 0.0, 0.25, 0.5, and 0.75 (seed 42 plus the severity index, Box-Muller sampling). Labels are never perturbed.
> - MAE and RMSE (lower is better) and R-squared (R2 = 1 - SS_res/SS_tot with SS_tot taken about the test-set mean; higher is better) are computed per (severity, method) on the perturbed test set.
> - The data are split by a deterministic seeded shuffle (seed 42) into 80% training and 20% test rows; standardization statistics come from the training split only.

### 3.3 Design Rationale and Scope

Three design choices deserve emphasis. First, the pure-Python implementations (Gaussian elimination for the ridge normal equations; IRLS for Huber) remove dependence on external solver libraries, so every number is a function of the data, the seeds, and the contract alone. Second, no hyperparameter search is performed: $\lambda$, the $\delta$ multiplier, the IRLS iteration count, and the perturbation grid are frozen, so the study measures the estimators as specified rather than as tuned. Third, perturbation is applied to standardized test covariates only, isolating *evaluation-time* robustness of a fixed fitted predictor from *training-time* robustness; this is a narrower question than distributionally robust training, which is not attempted here (future work).

## 4. Experimental Design

**Datasets.** Two tabular regression datasets are used: airfoil_self_noise (aerodynamic self-noise prediction) and concrete_strength, whose target is concrete compressive strength in MPa. Each dataset is processed independently under the protocol of Section 3.2 (80/20 seeded shuffle split, seed 42; training-split standardization).

**Methods under study and reference predictor.** Ridge and Huber regression are the two *methods under comparison*, not baselines. The only reference predictor executed is the training-set mean predictor, which emits $\bar{y}_{\mathrm{tr}}$ for every test row regardless of covariates; because it never reads the covariates, its metrics are invariant to perturbation severity and are reported once per dataset. This reference is deliberately weak and serves as a *sanity check* — a floor establishing that covariate-reading models still extract signal under perturbation — not as a meaningful comparator for the rank-reversal claims. No stronger in-domain baseline (e.g., OLS, Lasso, gradient boosting) and no alternative robust estimator (e.g., Theil–Sen, RANSAC) was executed; these are explicitly deferred to future work, and the reversals reported below are contextualized only within the executed pair.

**Conditions.** The factorial design crosses {ridge, Huber} × {0.0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75} severity per dataset, yielding four (severity, method) cells per dataset plus one reference row per dataset, and three metrics per cell. The pre-specified primary endpoint is the severity-0 ridge MAE (`0_0_ridge_mae`). All noise draws are deterministic (seed 42 plus severity index; Box–Muller sampling), so the design has zero run-to-run variance by construction; the absence of replicate seeds is addressed as a limitation in Section 7.

**Ablation structure.** Three ablation axes are built into the design: (a) *severity ablation* — the sweep from 0.0 to 0.75 isolates the marginal effect of covariate noise; (b) *method ablation* — ridge versus Huber under an identical pipeline isolates the effect of the loss function (both fitted on identical standardized features, with Huber initialized from the ridge solution); and (c) *metric ablation* — the same predictions scored under MAE, RMSE, and $R^2$ isolate the effect of the scoring rule on rankings. Ablations over $\lambda$, the $\delta$ multiplier, the IRLS iteration count, or a finer severity grid were **not** executed and are listed as future work. No cross-dataset pooling is performed; each dataset's cells are analyzed on their own terms.

## 5. Expected Results

The following expectations were formulated *prior to* execution and are recorded here explicitly as hypotheses, not observations; they are assessed against the observed outcomes in Section 6.

**H1 (reference-predictor dominance).** Both ridge and Huber regression should outperform the mean-predictor reference on all three metrics at every severity, because even perturbed covariates retain partial signal. Anticipated margin: large at severity 0.0 (roughly a third to a half of the reference error) and shrinking but positive at severity 0.75.

**H2 (monotone degradation).** Error metrics should increase (and $R^2$ decrease) monotonically with severity for both methods, since perturbation variance adds irreducibly to prediction error under a linear predictor: if $\hat{f}(x) = \beta_0 + \tilde{x}^{\top}\beta$, then $\hat{f}(\tilde{x} + \varepsilon) - \hat{f}(\tilde{x}) = \varepsilon^{\top}\beta$, a zero-mean perturbation whose dispersion grows linearly in severity (internal reasoning). Anticipated magnitude: a 10–30% MAE increase from severity 0.0 to 0.75.

**H3 (loss–metric alignment).** Huber's linear (rather than quadratic) penalty on large residuals should favor it on MAE, while ridge's least-squares objective should favor it on RMSE and $R^2$, particularly as perturbation inflates the residual scale and pushes more residuals past the Huber threshold $\delta$ (internal reasoning).

**H4 (rank stability).** If H3 holds, the *cross-method* ranking could be metric-dependent but was expected to be *severity-stable* within a metric: the Huber–ridge gap was anticipated to scale smoothly with severity without changing sign, because both predictors are linear in the same covariates and inherit the same input noise.

Section 6 shows that H1 and (partially) H3 were supported, H2 was violated on concrete_strength in MAE, and H4 was violated on both datasets in MAE — constituting the central empirical finding of this bounded study.

### 6.3 Summary of Rank Stability

Across the eight (dataset × metric) ranking tracks, six are severity-stable (ridge leads RMSE and R² on both datasets at all four severities) and two exhibit reversals (MAE on both datasets, with reversal thresholds of 0.25 for airfoil_self_noise and 0.75 for concrete_strength). These results show that (i) rankings under covariate perturbation are metric-dependent in a manner consistent with loss–metric alignment (H3), (ii) MAE rankings are severity-unstable on both executed datasets, and (iii) reversal thresholds do not transfer across datasets.

## 7. Discussion

**Interpretation.** The stable ridge advantage on RMSE and R² is consistent with the alignment between the least-squares objective and squared-error scoring (internal reasoning): as severity inflates residual dispersion, Huber's down-weighting of large residuals becomes increasingly costly under quadratic scoring, which matches the widening ridge margins on both datasets. Conversely, Huber's MAE advantage at low severity erodes and reverses as more residuals fall beyond the threshold $\delta$, and the reversal point differs by dataset, plausibly reflecting dataset-specific residual scales and signal-to-noise characteristics (internal reasoning). The non-monotone MAE trajectory on concrete_strength is a seed-specific residual-redistribution effect and is reported as such.

**Limitations — baseline adequacy.** The mean predictor is a sanity check, not a meaningful comparator; it establishes only that covariate signal survives perturbation (H1). Ridge and Huber are the two methods under study, not baselines, and no stronger in-domain baseline (OLS, Lasso, gradient boosting), no alternative robust estimator (Theil–Sen, RANSAC), and no ablations over lambda, delta, or a finer severity grid were executed. The rank reversals are therefore contextualized solely within the executed pair; they do not establish how either estimator would rank against a broader model set under shift.

**Limitations — scope and novelty.** Comparing ridge against Huber under covariate noise is a classical robust-statistics question with decades of prior work (internal reasoning); this study contributes a seeded, deterministic, fully auditable empirical observation on two datasets and two linear estimators — an incremental empirical note, not a new method, theory, or analysis technique. Generalization beyond the two executed datasets is expressly not claimed.

**Limitations — design.** The design uses one deterministic seed per severity; the observed reversals involve small gaps (e.g., 0.001471 on airfoil MAE at severity 0.25), and replicate seeds or analytic variance estimates would be required to separate reversals from finite-sample jitter, though determinism guarantees the reported values are exactly reproducible. Hyperparameters were frozen (lambda = 1.0, delta = 1.345 × MAD scale, 50 IRLS iterations); tuned settings could shift the rankings, and that axis is intentionally excluded. Perturbations are additive and Gaussian on standardized test features only; other shift families remain future work.

**Broader impact and ethics.** The societal benefit of the study is a caution: practitioners selecting a regressor by MAE at one noise level may deploy the wrong model at another, and single-metric selection under shift is demonstrably fragile. The corresponding risk is over-generalization — the two executed datasets do not license claims about all tabular tasks, and single-seed reversals should be treated as flags for further testing, not verdicts. Deterministic, library-free execution (protocol, Section 3.2) supports auditability, which is itself a public good for benchmarking practice.

## 8. Conclusion

This paper executed the frozen `tabular_robust_regression_v1` capability: pure-Python ridge regression (lambda = 1.0, closed form) and Huber regression (IRLS, delta = 1.345 × MAD scale, 50 iterations) evaluated under seeded Gaussian covariate perturbations at severities 0.0–0.75 against a training-mean reference on airfoil_self_noise and concrete_strength. The executed comparison showed that the MAE ranking between these two estimators reverses with severity — between severities 0.0 and 0.25 on airfoil_self_noise and between 0.5 and 0.75 on concrete_strength — while RMSE and R² rankings are stable at every severity, favoring ridge with widening margins; a non-monotone MAE response at severity 0.75 on concrete_strength further shows that MAE and RMSE can move in opposite directions under covariate perturbation. These observed outcomes are attributed specifically to the executed ridge and Huber implementations under the frozen contract, with the mean predictor serving only as a signal-floor sanity check. Future work should replicate the sweep across seeds, add stronger baselines and alternative robust estimators (OLS, Lasso, gradient boosting, Theil–Sen, RANSAC), ablate lambda, delta, and the severity grid, and extend beyond additive Gaussian test-time perturbations before any general claim about rank stability under covariate shift is warranted.
