## Abstract

The rapidly advancing field of neuro-symbolic artificial intelligence promises to combine the robust pattern recognition capabilities of deep neural networks with the verifiable, compositional reasoning of formal logic. However, the current landscape suffers from a critical methodological bottleneck: the absence of a unified, standardized suite of benchmarks and evaluation metrics. Existing models are evaluated on highly domain-specific tasks—ranging from pathology image analysis to architectural schematic generation—which makes it nearly impossible to measure generalizable reasoning capabilities, compare the computational overhead of different integrations, or track general advancements against a common baseline. To bridge this gap, this proposal introduces Neuro-SymbolicBench, a standardized, multi-domain benchmark suite featuring three tiers of complexity: synthetic rule-based environments, semi-structured text-to-Knowledge Graph reasoning tasks, and real-world noisy domains such as clinical note reasoning. Alongside the curated data, we propose a comprehensive evaluation framework comprising a novel composite metric. This metric explicitly disentangles and scores neural perception accuracy, symbolic reasoning fidelity, and integration latency, allowing researchers to plot their systems on a multi-axis Pareto frontier of performance versus interpretability versus compute. The expected result is a publicly available, standardized evaluation protocol that drives rigorous, apples-to-apples comparisons, thereby accelerating the development of more scalable and robust hybrid AI systems.

## Introduction

The pursuit of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) demands AI systems that not only perceive the world with high accuracy but also reason about it in a human-like, interpretable manner. In recent years, deep neural networks, particularly Large Language Models (LLMs), have achieved unprecedented breakthroughs in statistical pattern recognition, perception, and natural language generation. However, these purely sub-symbolic systems remain fundamentally brittle. They are prone to hallucinations, struggle with systematic compositional generalization, and lack the capacity for verifiable, step-by-step logical deduction. Conversely, classical symbolic AI offers rigorous, interpretable reasoning grounded in formal logic but suffers from extreme brittleness to noise and an inability to learn from raw, high-dimensional data. Neuro-symbolic artificial intelligence has emerged as a promising paradigm to bridge this divide, seeking to integrate the perceptual prowess of neural networks with the logical rigor of symbolic systems. This hybrid approach is increasingly viewed as essential for deploying safe, reliable, and transparent AI in high-stakes real-world domains such as clinical healthcare, autonomous driving, and financial compliance.

Despite the theoretical promise and rapid proliferation of neuro-symbolic architectures, the field currently faces a severe methodological roadblock: the lack of a unified, standardized evaluation framework. As noted in recent structured reviews (Nawaz et al., 2025), existing neuro-symbolic models are overwhelmingly evaluated on highly domain-specific tasks. One research team might test their architecture on visual question answering, while another evaluates a completely different integration strategy on knowledge graph completion or fake news detection. This fragmentation makes it nearly impossible for the research community to conduct apples-to-apples comparisons. Consequently, fundamental questions remain unanswered. It is unclear which integration strategies actually yield the most generalizable reasoning capabilities, how much computational overhead different hybrid architectures introduce, and whether the symbolic components are truly executing faithful logic or merely acting as post-hoc rationalizations for neural intuitions. Without a common baseline, tracking the true scientific progress of hybrid AI is highly speculative.

To address this critical infrastructure gap, we propose Neuro-SymbolicBench, a comprehensive, multi-domain evaluation suite specifically designed for cross-domain comparison of hybrid AI systems. Our approach moves beyond traditional, single-axis accuracy metrics by introducing a multi-tiered benchmark environment that systematically increases in complexity and noise—from purely synthetic algorithmic puzzles to real-world clinical text. The key innovation of Neuro-SymbolicBench is its formalized, multi-dimensional evaluation protocol. We propose a novel composite metric that explicitly disentangles neural perception accuracy from symbolic reasoning fidelity, while simultaneously accounting for integration latency. By formally scoring these distinct capabilities, researchers will be able to plot their architectures on a multi-axis Pareto frontier, analyzing the inherent trade-offs between task performance, logical faithfulness, and computational overhead.

The expected contributions of this research are threefold. First, we will release a publicly available, standardized benchmark suite with pre-defined train/test splits explicitly tailored to stress-test the interface between neural perception and symbolic reasoning. Second, we will formalize a multi-dimensional evaluation protocol that provides a granular diagnostic of system capabilities and failure modes, moving beyond simple accuracy to measure reasoning faithfulness. Finally, we will validate this suite by running a diverse array of existing baseline architectures—ranging from standard LLMs to energy-based neuro-symbolic models—demonstrating the benchmark's ability to successfully differentiate between integration strategies and highlight known failure modes in dual-process systems.

## Related Work

## Related Work

The rapid proliferation of hybrid neuro-symbolic (NeSy) architectures has yielded diverse integration paradigms, ranging from tightly coupled neural theorem provers to loosely coupled systems utilizing large language models (LLMs) as semantic parsers. However, the evaluation of these paradigms remains highly fragmented. In this section, we situate our proposed *Neuro-SymbolicBench* within three key areas of existing literature: domain-specific NeSy evaluations, the limitations of standard benchmarks, and the evolution of multi-dimensional metrics.

**Domain-Specific Evaluations and the Fragmentation of NeSy Benchmarks**
Historically, neuro-symbolic systems have been evaluated in tightly constrained, domain-specific silos. For instance, in visual question answering and scene understanding, models like Neural-Symbolic Concept Learner (NSCL) and Neuro-Symbolic VQA (NS-VQA) (Yi et al., 2018) are evaluated almost exclusively on the CLEVR dataset (Johnson et al., 2017). While CLEVR effectively tests compositional visual reasoning, it offers little insight into a system's ability to handle the noise and ambiguity of real-world text. Conversely, systems designed for natural language processing and knowledge graph reasoning, such as those based on Logic Tensor Networks (Serafini & d'Avila Garcez, 2016) or neural theorem provers (Rocktäschel & Riedel, 2017), are typically evaluated on isolated knowledge base query answering (KBQA) tasks or synthetic logic puzzles (e.g., bAbI). More recently, LLM-based NeSy approaches have been tested on mathematical reasoning datasets like GSM8K (Cobbe et al., 2021). While these individual datasets effectively demonstrate specific capabilities, they prevent the community from conducting cross-domain, apples-to-apples comparisons. A system excelling at structured visual parsing cannot be easily compared to one excelling at noisy text-based logic, making it impossible to determine which underlying integration paradigm yields the most generalizable reasoning capabilities.

**The Need for Standardized, Tiered Complexity**
Recent meta-surveys and position papers have explicitly highlighted this evaluation crisis. In their critical analysis, *“Is Neuro-Symbolic AI Meeting its Promises in Natural Language Processing?”*, Marra et al. (2023) underscore that the lack of unified benchmarks severely hampers the measurement of generalizable reasoning and reasoning "faithfulness." Traditional deep learning benchmarks, such as SuperGLUE (Wang et al., 2019), prioritize end-task accuracy, implicitly encouraging neural networks to exploit spurious statistical correlations rather than engage in rigorous logical deduction. Furthermore, standard benchmarks fail to provide the structural scaffolding necessary to evaluate the symbolic components of hybrid systems. *Neuro-SymbolicBench* directly addresses this by introducing a tiered evaluation suite—spanning synthetic rule-based environments, semi-structured text-to-KG reasoning, and real-world noisy domains (e.g., clinical notes). While recent efforts like the Abstract Reasoning Challenge (ARC) (Chollet, 2019) have introduced synthetic tasks designed to resist rote memorization, they do not explicitly disentangle neural perception from symbolic execution, nor do they span the multi-domain continuum required to stress-test hybrid AI.

**Multi-Dimensional Metrics and Pareto Frontiers**
Beyond the data itself, the metrics used to evaluate NeSy systems remain fundamentally inadequate. Standard evaluation relies almost exclusively on task accuracy (e.g., exact match or F1 score). However, as Garcez et al. (2019) and recent studies on LLM reasoning (Turquoise et al., 2023) point out, accuracy alone cannot capture the multifaceted objectives of neuro-symbolic AI, which explicitly trades off performance for interpretability and computational overhead. Some recent works have introduced targeted metrics, such as Trustful AI metrics for measuring concept alignment (Barbiero et al., 2023) or formal verification scores for specific logic constraints. However, these metrics are rarely unified. To date, there is no standardized protocol that explicitly disentangles neural perception accuracy, symbolic reasoning fidelity, and integration latency. *Neuro-SymbolicBench* fills this gap by proposing a formalized, multi-dimensional evaluation protocol. By scoring systems across these distinct axes, we enable researchers to plot their architectures on a multi-axis Pareto frontier of performance versus interpretability versus compute, establishing a rigorous, standardized baseline for the next generation of hybrid AI.

## Proposed Method

The core objective of Neuro-SymbolicBench is to establish a rigorous, reproducible, and mathematically grounded framework for evaluating neuro-symbolic systems. We formalize the evaluation paradigm by defining the components of a hybrid architecture, introducing a multi-tiered data suite, and proposing a novel composite metric that disentangles the distinct computational phases of a neuro-symbolic system.

**Formal Problem Definition**
Let a standard neuro-symbolic system be defined as a tuple $\mathcal{M} = (\mathcal{N}, \mathcal{S}, \mathcal{I})$, where $\mathcal{N}$ represents the neural perception module, $\mathcal{S}$ represents the symbolic reasoning module, and $\mathcal{I}$ represents the integration interface (e.g., a neural-to-symbolic parser or a differentiable logic layer). Given an input $x$ (e.g., raw text or an image), the neural module generates a latent representation $z = \mathcal{N}(x; \theta)$. The integration interface then maps this latent representation into a symbolic representation or program $p = \mathcal{I}(z)$. Finally, the symbolic reasoning engine executes this program against a knowledge base $\mathcal{K}$ to produce a final answer $y = \mathcal{S}(p, \mathcal{K})$. 

The goal of our evaluation framework is to assess the system not just on the correctness of $y$, but on the fidelity of the intermediate steps. We define the ground truth tuple for a given input $x$ as $(y^{*}, p^{*}, z^{*})$, representing the true target label, the true symbolic program, and the true perceptual representation, respectively.

**Multi-Tiered Data Suite**
To ensure generalizability, the benchmark is structured into three distinct tiers of escalating noise and complexity:
1. **Tier 1: Synthetic Rule-Based Environments.** We algorithmically generate logical puzzles (e.g., variations of the bAbI tasks and algorithmically generated deductive logic grids). These tasks contain zero perceptual noise, allowing for the pure isolation of symbolic reasoning fidelity.
2. **Tier 2: Semi-Structured Text-to-KG Reasoning.** We curate modified Knowledge Base Question Answering (KBQA) datasets, such as ComplexWebQuestions and MetaQA, augmented with explicit logic constraints. This tier tests the system's ability to extract formal logic from naturally ambiguous human language.
3. **Tier 3: Real-World Noisy Domains.** We utilize clinical note reasoning datasets (e.g., MedQA, clinical NLI) where the input is highly unstructured, and the knowledge base is incomplete. This tests the robustness of the integration interface under real-world distribution shifts.

**Multi-Dimensional Composite Metric**
The central methodological contribution is a novel composite metric designed to disentangle the neural and symbolic components. We define the Neuro-Symbolic Score (NSS) as a convex combination of three sub-scores:

$$ \text{NSS} = \alpha \cdot \text{Acc}_{\text{task}} + \beta \cdot \text{Fidelity}_{\text{sym}} + \gamma \cdot \text{Efficiency}_{\text{int}} $$

where $\alpha, \beta, \gamma \in [0, 1]$ are weighting hyperparameters such that $\alpha + \beta + \gamma = 1$. 

1. **Task Accuracy ($\text{Acc}_{\text{task}}$):** This is the standard exact match accuracy between the predicted output $y$ and the ground truth $y^{*}$:
$$ \text{Acc}_{\text{task}} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \mathbb{I}(y_i = y_i^{*}) $$

2. **Symbolic Reasoning Fidelity ($\text{Fidelity}_{\text{sym}}$):** Standard accuracy is insufficient because a neural network might guess the correct answer $y$ via statistical shortcuts while executing a completely flawed symbolic program $p$. To measure reasoning faithfulness, we compute the program alignment:
$$ \text{Fidelity}_{\text{sym}} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \text{Sim}(p_i, p_i^{*}) $$
where $\text{Sim}(\cdot, \cdot)$ is an Abstract Syntax Tree (AST) similarity metric measuring the structural overlap between the predicted logic program and the ground truth logic program. 

3. **Integration Efficiency ($\text{Efficiency}_{\text{int}}$):** To capture the computational overhead of hybridization, we measure the relative latency of the symbolic integration. We compute an efficiency score normalized over a baseline pure neural forward pass time $T_{\mathcal{N}}$:
$$ \text{Efficiency}_{\text{int}} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \exp\left( - \lambda \frac{T_{\mathcal{I}}(x_i) + T_{\mathcal{S}}(p_i)}{T_{\mathcal{N}}(x_i)} \right) $$
where $\lambda$ is a scaling factor. This heavily penalizes architectures that introduce massive computational bottlenecks for marginal reasoning gains.

## Expected Contributions

The proposed research yields the following specific, measurable contributions to the neuro-symbolic AI community:

1. **A Standardized, Multi-Tiered Benchmark Suite:** The public release of Neuro-SymbolicBench, comprising three distinct tiers (synthetic, semi-structured, and real-world noisy domains) with pre-defined, rigorously curated train/test splits. This matters because it eliminates the current fragmentation in the field, providing a single, standardized testbed that enables direct, apples-to-apples comparisons across different hybrid architectures.
2. **A Formalized Multi-Dimensional Evaluation Protocol:** The introduction and mathematical formalization of the Neuro-Symbolic Score (NSS), a novel composite metric that explicitly disentangles neural perception accuracy, symbolic reasoning fidelity, and integration latency. This matters because standard accuracy metrics often mask underlying reasoning failures; the NSS forces researchers to optimize for logical faithfulness and computational practicality alongside task performance.
3. **Comprehensive Empirical Analysis of Baseline Architectures:** A large-scale empirical study evaluating a diverse array of existing paradigms (standard LLMs, pure symbolic solvers, MRKL-style pipelines, and energy-based models) on the new benchmark. This matters because it will provide the first unified leaderboard for hybrid AI, mapping the current frontier of the field and establishing clear baselines for future architectural innovations.
4. **Open-Source Evaluation Toolkit:** The release of an open-source Python library containing the data loaders, symbolic parsers, and metric calculation algorithms required to run the NSS evaluation. This ensures reproducibility and lowers the barrier to entry for other researchers attempting to evaluate their neuro-symbolic systems.

## Evaluation Plan

**Datasets**
The benchmark draws from publicly available datasets, modified to include explicit symbolic ground truth programs, mapped across the three tiers:
- **Tier 1 (Synthetic):** *bAbI Tasks* (Weston et al., 2015) and *CLUTRR* (Sinha et al., 2019). Size: ~10k synthetic samples. Source: `https://github.com/facebookarchive/bAbI-tasks`, `https://github.com/rowancassidy/clutrr`.
- **Tier 2 (Semi-structured):** *ComplexWebQuestions* (Talmor & Berant, 2018) and *MetaQA* (Zhang et al., 2018). Size: ~35k questions. Source: `https://www.dropbox.com/s/56ep57guj9g7o4b/ComplexWebQuestions_train.txt`.
- **Tier 3 (Real-world):** *MedQA* (Jin et al., 2021) and *Clinical NLI* (Romanov & Shivade, 2018). Size: ~15k clinical instances. Source: `https://github.com/jind11/MedQA`, `https://github.com/ncbi-nlp/ClinicalNLI`.

**Baselines**
To validate the benchmark's ability to differentiate integration strategies, we evaluate the following representative baselines:
1. **Standard LLMs (Pure Neural):** LLaMA-3-8B and GPT-4o evaluated via standard prompting. Represents sub-symbolic reasoning without explicit symbolic integration (Touvron et al., 2023).
2. **Pure Symbolic Solvers:** Logic-based engines (e.g., Prolog, Z3) provided with perfectly parsed inputs. Represents the upper bound of reasoning fidelity but the lower bound of perceptual robustness.
3. **MRKL-Style Systems:** Modular neuro-symbolic pipelines utilizing an LLM to route queries to external symbolic tools (e.g., calculators, knowledge graphs), representing the dominant industry approach to hybrid AI (Karpas et al., 2022).
4. **Differentiable Neuro-Symbolic Models:** DeepProbLog (Manhaeve et al., 2018) and neural theorem provers, representing tightly coupled, end-to-end differentiable logic integration.

**Metrics**
Systems are evaluated using the proposed Neuro-Symbolic Score (NSS) framework:
- **Task Accuracy ($\text{Acc}_{\text{task}}$):** Exact match accuracy of the final answer.
- **Symbolic Reasoning Fidelity ($\text{Fidelity}_{\text{sym}}$):** AST (Abstract Syntax Tree) similarity between the generated logic program and the ground-truth logic program.
- **Integration Efficiency ($\text{Efficiency}_{\text{int}}$):** Normalized computational latency ratio between the symbolic overhead and the neural forward pass.
- **Neuro-Symbolic Score (NSS):** The aggregate composite score ($\alpha=0.4, \beta=0.4, \gamma=0.2$) plotted on a 3D Pareto frontier.

**Ablation Design**
1. **Ablation on Perception Noise:** We artificially inject varying levels of Gaussian noise into the input embeddings of Tier 1 tasks to measure the degradation curve of symbolic fidelity as neural perception fails. This tests the cascading error robustness of different integration interfaces.
2. **Ablation on Knowledge Base Completeness:** For Tier 2 tasks, we randomly remove varying percentages (10%, 30%, 50%) of the facts from the underlying Knowledge Graphs. This ablation tests how gracefully different neuro-symbolic baselines handle incomplete symbolic environments versus pure LLMs.

## Risk Mitigation

**Risk 1: Conceptual Difficulty of Disentanglement.** Designing a composite metric that accurately and fairly disentangles neural perception from symbolic reasoning across highly diverse architectures might prove conceptually difficult and face community pushback. 
*Mitigation Strategy:* We will ground the metric formulation in formal Abstract Syntax Tree (AST) analysis, providing rigorous mathematical proofs of metric boundedness and symmetry. We will host an open community workshop prior to publication to gather feedback on the weighting scheme.
*Fallback Plan:* If the community deems the single NSS score too rigid, we will decouple the metric. We will report the three sub-metrics (Accuracy, Fidelity, Efficiency) exclusively as a multi-objective Pareto frontier, allowing researchers to apply their own domain-specific weights.

**Risk 2: Standardization Across Diverse Paradigms.** Achieving true 'apples-to-apples' standardization across wildly different neuro-symbolic integration paradigms (e.g., energy-based models vs. MRKL-style pipelines) could be challenging due to differing input/output assumptions.
*Mitigation Strategy:* We will define highly abstracted API interfaces in our open-source toolkit. By utilizing a universal intermediate representation (e.g., standardizing all symbolic outputs into a generic logic programming format), we can normalize the evaluation across disparate architectures.
*Fallback Plan:* If a specific architecture (e.g., spectral graph models) cannot map to the intermediate representation, we will create distinct "tracks" within the benchmark (e.g., tightly-coupled vs. loosely-coupled tracks) to ensure fair comparison within architectural families.

**Risk 3: Data Licensing and Privacy Constraints.** Licensing and privacy constraints, particularly regarding the real-world noisy domains like clinical notes (Tier 3), may restrict the ability to release a fully unified, publicly available benchmark.
*Mitigation Strategy:* We will strictly utilize datasets that are already publicly available under permissive licenses (e.g., MedQA, Clinical NLI) and rely on de-identified data, avoiding any institutional review board (IRB) bottlenecks.
*Fallback Plan:* If specific clinical datasets pose distribution restrictions, we will replace Tier 3 with synthetic clinical notes generated via advanced LLMs (e.g., GPT-4) that mimic the noise and structure of real clinical text without containing Protected Health Information (PHI).

## Title

# Guardian Angels: Uncertainty-Aware Neural Gates for Mitigating Cascading Errors in Dual-Process Systems

The title "Guardian Angels: Uncertainty-Aware Neural Gates for Mitigating Cascading Errors in Dual-Process Systems" encapsulates the core innovation and metaphorical framing of our proposed research. The "Guardian Angel" nomenclature is deliberately chosen to evoke the concept of a protective, oversight mechanism that operates silently in the background—intervening only when necessary to prevent catastrophic failure. This metaphor accurately reflects the architectural design, wherein the GA module remains passive during normal operation but activates decisively when calibrated uncertainty signals indicate potential errors in the neural component's latent representations. The phrase "Uncertainty-Aware Neural Gates" specifies the technical mechanism through which this protection is achieved, emphasizing the integration of predictive uncertainty estimation as a gating criterion. "Mitigating Cascading Errors" identifies the primary failure mode being addressed—the propagation and amplification of initial misperceptions through subsequent symbolic reasoning steps. Finally, "Dual-Process Systems" situates the contribution within the broader theoretical framework of cognitive science-inspired AI architectures, signaling both the interdisciplinary foundations and the specific domain of application for the proposed intervention.

## Abstract

Dual-process neuro-symbolic systems, which integrate fast neural intuition (System 1) with rigorous symbolic reflection (System 2), represent a leading paradigm for complex multi-step reasoning. However, these architectures remain highly susceptible to cascading errors: if the neural perception module generates a fundamentally flawed latent representation, the downstream symbolic solver lacks the grounding to detect or recover from the error, often resulting in confidently incorrect outputs. This paper proposes the Guardian Angel (GA) architecture, an uncertainty-aware intermediary gating mechanism that intercepts flawed representations before they propagate to the symbolic reasoning engine. By leveraging calibrated predictive uncertainties via Monte Carlo Dropout and Deep Ensembles, the GA module evaluates the reliability of the neural output. When uncertainty exceeds a learned threshold, the GA triggers targeted fallback mechanisms, such as abductive reflection loops or external knowledge base queries, to correct the perception. Evaluated on perturbed multi-step reasoning datasets, the expected result is a significant reduction in logical consistency failures and hallucinations, establishing a robust, fail-safe mechanism for next-generation neuro-symbolic systems.

## Introduction

The pursuit of artificial general intelligence has increasingly converged on neuro-symbolic architectures, which elegantly synthesize the rapid, pattern-matching intuition of deep learning (System 1) with the deliberate, rule-based rigor of symbolic reasoning (System 2). This dual-process paradigm promises robust multi-step reasoning capabilities, enabling models to traverse complex logical dependencies while maintaining the semantic flexibility required to process unstructured, real-world data. By coupling neural perception with symbolic execution, these systems have achieved state-of-the-art performance on challenging benchmarks in mathematical reasoning, knowledge base querying, and logical deduction. The underlying assumption of this paradigm is strictly unidirectional: the neural module faithfully translates raw inputs into a structured latent representation, which the symbolic solver then flawlessly executes. However, this tight coupling exposes a critical and well-documented vulnerability. If the neural component generates a fundamentally flawed or hallucinated latent representation, the symbolic reflection mechanism often lacks the semantic grounding to detect or recover from the error. Consequently, the solver proceeds to execute logically sound operations on semantically invalid inputs, leading to confidently incorrect outputs and catastrophic cascading failures across multi-step reasoning chains.

Despite the recognized severity of this cascading error problem, current mitigation strategies remain insufficient, leaving dual-process systems highly brittle. Existing approaches typically attempt to address this issue by imposing hard constraints on the symbolic solver or relying on post-hoc verification mechanisms. However, these downstream interventions are fundamentally limited; once a perceptual error has been encoded into the initial system state, symbolic solvers lack the fluid contextual understanding necessary to identify the misalignment with the original raw input. The problem of cascading errors in dual-process neuro-symbolic systems is well-documented, yet the field lacks a formalized understanding of how predictive uncertainty in neural perception directly correlates with downstream symbolic reasoning failures. Current systems operate largely in a binary fashion—either proceeding with full confidence or failing outright—without a mechanism to gauge the reliability of the neural translation before it irrevocably corrupts the symbolic reasoning pipeline. 

To address this critical limitation, we propose the 'Guardian Angel' (GA) architecture, an uncertainty-aware neural gating mechanism designed to act as a robust intermediary between the neural and symbolic modules. Rather than attempting to fix errors post-hoc, the GA architecture intercepts potential failures at the precise boundary between perception and reasoning. In our framework, the neural module outputs its standard latent representation alongside calibrated predictive uncertainties, quantified via established techniques such as Monte Carlo Dropout or Deep Ensembles. The GA module continuously evaluates this uncertainty metric. If the predictive uncertainty exceeds a learned threshold, the GA intercepts the flawed representation before it enters the symbolic solver, effectively halting the cascade of errors. It then dynamically triggers a fallback mechanism tailored to the context of the failure. These fallbacks include targeted abductive reflection loops, queries for human-in-the-loop clarification, or retrieval from external knowledge sources, as commonly utilized in MRKL (Module, Reasoning, Knowledge, and Language) systems, to iteratively correct the perception.

The contributions of this research are threefold. First, we introduce a robust, uncertainty-aware architecture that significantly reduces hallucinations and logical failures in multi-step reasoning by acting as an intelligent, preventative gate rather than a reactive patch. Second, we provide a formalized understanding of how predictive uncertainty in neural perception correlates with symbolic reasoning failures, bridging a critical theoretical gap in the neuro-symbolic literature. Finally, we demonstrate the high practical viability of this approach across complex reasoning tasks. By leveraging publicly accessible datasets—such as ComplexWebQuestions, GSM8K, and AQuA—and synthesizing targeted perturbations, we establish a rigorous benchmark for evaluating cascading error mitigation. Ultimately, the Guardian Angel framework transforms predictive uncertainty from a mere statistical byproduct into an actionable safeguard, paving the way for more reliable, transparent, and trustworthy neuro-symbolic AI systems.

Recent efforts to bridge this gap, such as the LAMBADA framework (Kazemi et al., 2023), have focused on iterative refinement. However, these systems often lack an explicit mechanism to halt execution when the initial perception is fundamentally flawed, leading them to attempt logical deduction over nonsensical premises. 

**Uncertainty Estimation in Deep Learning.** To build resilience against neural perception failures, our work draws upon calibrated predictive uncertainty. Standard deep neural networks are notoriously overconfident in their predictions (Guo et al., 2017). To mitigate this, techniques such as Monte Carlo (MC) Dropout (Gal & Ghahramani, 2016) and Deep Ensembles (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017) have become standard for quantifying epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty. While uncertainty estimation has been successfully applied to identify out-of-distribution inputs or trigger selective prediction (Geifman & El-Yaniv, 2017), its application as an active gating mechanism within neuro-symbolic pipelines remains underexplored. Existing methods typically use uncertainty to abstain from answering entirely, rather than as a diagnostic signal to trigger corrective, symbolic sub-routines.

**Cascading Errors and Modular Intercession.** The problem of error propagation across heterogeneous modules is well-documented in pipeline architectures. In standard neural module networks, earlier mistakes irreversibly corrupt downstream computations (Subramanian et al., 2020). The concept of an intermediary "guardian" or monitor aligns with recent explorations into neuro-symbolic error correction, such as neuro-symbolic concept learners that abduce corrections for inconsistent programs (Mao et al., 2019). Furthermore, our proposed fallback mechanisms resonate with MRKL systems (Karpas et al., 2022) and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (Lewis et al., 2020), which query external knowledge bases to fill representational gaps. 

**Positioning and Novelty.** Despite these advancements, existing dual-process systems treat neural perception and symbolic reasoning as tightly coupled sequential processes without an intervening "circuit breaker." The proposed 'Guardian Angel' (GA) architecture demonstrates its primary novelty not through the invention of isolated components, but through a highly synergistic combination of existing techniques. By routing calibrated neural uncertainties into an explicit gating mechanism, the GA architecture operationalizes uncertainty estimates as dynamic circuit breakers. Unlike standard selective prediction, which simply abstains, our architecture actively intercepts flawed latent representations to trigger targeted abductive reflection loops or human-in-the-loop clarification. Consequently, this work provides a formalized understanding of how predictive uncertainty correlates with downstream symbolic reasoning failures, offering a highly feasible and practical architectural intervention to mitigate cascading hallucinations in complex reasoning tasks.

## Proposed Method

The proposed Guardian Angel (GA) architecture introduces an uncertainty-aware intermediary gate between the neural perception module (System 1) and the symbolic reasoning engine (System 2). This section details the formal problem definition, the architectural components, the mathematical formulations governing the gating mechanism, and the fallback routing strategy.

**Problem Definition.** 
Let a dual-process neuro-symbolic system be defined by the tuple $(f_\theta, S, \mathcal{X}, \mathcal{Y})$, where $f_\theta$ is a neural network parameterized by $\theta$ that maps an input space $\mathcal{X}$ to a latent representation space $\mathcal{Z}$, and $S$ is a symbolic solver that maps $\mathcal{Z}$ to an output space $\mathcal{Y}$. In standard neuro-symbolic pipelines, the prediction $\hat{y}$ for a given input $x$ is computed sequentially as $\hat{y} = S(f_\theta(x))$. 

A cascading error occurs when the neural network produces a flawed latent representation $z' = f_\theta(x)$ such that $z' \notin \mathcal{Z}_{true}$, where $\mathcal{Z}_{true}$ is the set of representations that would lead to a correct final output. The symbolic solver blindly executes $S(z')$, resulting in an erroneous prediction $\hat{y} \neq y$. Our objective is to learn an intermediary gating function $g_\phi: \mathcal{Z} \rightarrow \{0, 1\}$, parameterized by $\phi$, that intercepts $z'$. If $g_\phi(z') = 1$, the representation is passed to $S$. If $g_\phi(z') = 0$, the representation is blocked, and a fallback function $\mathcal{F}(x, z')$ is triggered to correct the perception.

**Uncertainty Estimation.**
To inform the gating function $g_\phi$, we require a robust measure of the neural network's predictive uncertainty. We utilize Monte Carlo (MC) Dropout. For a given input $x$, we perform $T$ stochastic forward passes through $f_\theta$, where dropout is active during inference. The predictive distribution is approximated as:
$$ p(y|x, \theta) \approx \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^T p(y|x, \theta_t) $$
The latent representation $z_t$ and predictive probability vector $p_t$ are captured for each pass $t$. We compute the mean latent representation $\bar{z} = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T z_t$ and the predictive entropy $\mathcal{H}$ as our primary uncertainty metric $\mathcal{U}(x)$:
$$ \mathcal{U}(x) = - \sum_{c=1}^C \bar{p}_c \log \bar{p}_c $$
where $\bar{p}_c = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T p_{t,c}$ is the average probability for class $c$.

**The Guardian Angel Gating Mechanism.**
The GA module evaluates the uncertainty $\mathcal{U}(x)$ against a dynamic threshold $\tau$. Unlike static thresholds, $\tau$ is adapted based on the local validation set performance to maintain a target false-positive rate (blocking correct predictions). The gating decision is formally defined as:
$$ g_\phi(x) = \begin{cases} 
1 & \text{if } \mathcal{U}(x) \leq \tau \text{ (Confident)} \\
0 & \text{if } \mathcal{U}(x) > \tau \text{ (Uncertain)}
\end{cases} $$
If $g_\phi(x) = 1$, the system proceeds with standard neuro-symbolic inference: $\hat{y} = S(\bar{z})$. If $g_\phi(x) = 0$, the GA module blocks $\bar{z}$ from entering the symbolic solver and triggers the fallback routing mechanism.

**Fallback Routing Strategy.**
When the GA gate intercepts an uncertain representation, the system must recover gracefully. We implement a tiered fallback strategy $\mathcal{F}(x, \bar{z})$:
1.  *Targeted Abductive Reflection:* If $\mathcal{U}(x)$ is moderately uncertain ($\tau < \mathcal{U}(x) \leq \tau + \delta$), the system attempts to locally perturb $\bar{z}$ to find the nearest valid symbolic input that satisfies logical constraints, minimizing the repair distance.
2.  *External Knowledge Query (MRKL):* If $\mathcal{U}(x)$ is highly uncertain ($\mathcal{U}(x) > \tau + \delta$), the system queries an external knowledge base (e.g., Wikidata) or uses a tool-retrieval augmentation to ground the perception, replacing $\bar{z}$ with a knowledge-grounded representation $z_{kb}$.
3.  *Abstention:* If fallback mechanisms fail to yield a confident representation, the system outputs an abstention token, preventing a confidently incorrect hallucination.

**Training Objective.**
The neural network $f_\theta$ is trained with a composite loss function that jointly optimizes for predictive accuracy and uncertainty calibration. We employ a Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL) loss combined with a calibration-aware Brier Score penalty:
$$ \mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{NLL} + \lambda \cdot \mathcal{L}_{Brier} $$
$$ \mathcal{L}_{Brier} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \sum_{c=1}^C (p_{i,c} - \mathbb{1}_{y_i, c})^2 $$
where $\lambda$ is a hyperparameter controlling the weight of the calibration penalty. This ensures that the uncertainty metric $\mathcal{U}(x)$ is highly reliable for the GA gate's thresholding logic.

**Baselines**
We will compare the GA-augmented system against the following baselines:

2.  **Abductive Reflection (AR)**: The closest prior work, which reactively attempts to rectify symbolic inconsistencies after they occur by searching for minimal adjustments to the neural perception.
3.  **End-to-End Differentiable Neuro-Symbolic (DeepProbLog)**: A baseline that replaces strict symbolic logic with continuous relaxations, evaluating whether differentiability inherently prevents cascading failures at the cost of logical strictness.

**Metrics**
We will measure performance using the following metrics:
1.  **Final Answer Accuracy ($Acc$)**: The primary metric, measuring the proportion of correct final outputs. $Acc = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \mathbb{1}(\hat{y}_i = y_i)$.
2.  **Logical Consistency Score ($LCS$)**: Measures the proportion of final answers that, while potentially incorrect, adhere to the strict logical constraints of the symbolic solver without hallucination. $LCS = \frac{\text{Number of logically valid outputs}}{N}$.
3.  **Cascading Error Rate ($CER$)**: The core metric for this proposal, measuring the frequency with which a neural misclassification leads to a catastrophic logical failure. $CER = \frac{\text{Neural errors causing logical failures}}{\text{Total neural errors}}$.
4.  **Area Under the Risk-Coverage Curve (AURC)**: Evaluates the calibration of the uncertainty gate by measuring the system's ability to abstain from uncertain inputs while maintaining high accuracy on confident ones.

**Ablation Design**
To isolate the impact of the GA module, we propose the following ablation experiments:
1.  **Static vs. Dynamic Thresholding**: We will ablate the dynamic threshold $\tau$ by replacing it with a fixed, static threshold. This will demonstrate the necessity of adaptive calibration in diverse input distributions.
2.  **Fallback Mechanism Ablation**: We will individually disable the three fallback pathways (Abductive Reflection, External KB Query, and Abstention) to measure the contribution of each tier to the overall error recovery process.
3.  **Uncertainty Source Ablation**: We will replace MC Dropout with a deterministic uncertainty estimation method (e.g., Single-shot entropy) to evaluate the importance of stochastic Bayesian approximation for the GA gate's effectiveness.

## Risk Mitigation

**Risk 1: Poor Uncertainty Calibration.** 
A primary feasibility concern is that predictive uncertainties from MC Dropout may be poorly calibrated, leading to high false-positive rates where the GA gate blocks correct predictions, unnecessarily degrading overall accuracy. 
*Mitigation:* We will employ joint loss optimization (NLL + Brier Score) during the neural module's training phase specifically to enhance calibration. Furthermore, we will implement temperature scaling on a held-out validation set to flatten the predictive distribution before thresholding.
*Fallback Plan:* If calibration remains insufficient, we will pivot from MC Dropout to Deep Ensembles, which consistently provide superior uncertainty estimates at the cost of increased compute, or explore deterministic uncertainty methods like SNGP (Spectral-normalized Neural Gaussian Process).

**Risk 2: Integration Latency.** 
Introducing an intermediary gating mechanism, stochastic MC Dropout sampling ($T$ forward passes), and potential fallback loops (like external API queries) could drastically increase inference latency, rendering the system impractical for real-time applications.
*Mitigation:* We will limit MC Dropout passes to a small, empirically validated number (e.g., $T=5$ or $10$). We will also implement asynchronous pre-fetching for the external knowledge base queries and set strict computational budgets (timeouts) for the abductive reflection loop.
*Fallback Plan:* If latency remains prohibitive, we will replace the iterative abductive reflection with a single-shot, trained "corrector" neural network that approximates the repair, or transition to a single-pass deterministic uncertainty gate.

**Risk 3: Fallback Loop Complexity.** 
Designing and tuning the fallback mechanisms—specifically deciding when to trigger human-in-the-loop vs. an external knowledge base query vs. an abductive reflection loop—adds significant system complexity that might be difficult to evaluate systematically within the project timeline.
*Mitigation:* We will utilize strict, non-overlapping uncertainty bands ($\tau$, $\tau + \delta$) to deterministically route fallbacks, removing ambiguous routing decisions. We will initially exclude human-in-the-loop to constrain the scope, focusing purely on automated algorithmic recovery.
*Fallback Plan:* If the tiered routing proves too complex to tune, we will simplify the architecture to a binary gate: pass the representation to the symbolic solver if confident, or completely abstain from answering if uncertain. This preserves the core contribution of preventing cascading errors while drastically reducing engineering overhead.
